Authentication ===========
Authentication is the process of verifying a user's identity. It answers the question: "Who are you?" Common methods of authentication include:

Passwords: Users provide a password matching their account.
Biometrics: Fingerprints, facial recognition, or other physical traits verify identity.
Multi-factor Authentication (MFA): Combines two or more authentication factors (e.g., password + SMS code).
In an application, authentication typically involves logging in with credentials to confirm the user's identity before granting access to the system.

Authorization ============
Authorization, on the other hand, determines what a user is allowed to do within the system. It answers the question: "What are you allowed to do?" Once authenticated, the system checks the user’s permissions to decide what resources or actions are accessible. For example:

Access Levels: Admin, editor, and viewer roles with different permissions.
Resource Access: Specifying which files, databases, or actions a user can access based on their role.


Authorization generally follows authentication; first, a user’s identity is verified, then their permissions are checked.
